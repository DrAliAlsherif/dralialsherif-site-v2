AI Prompt Engineering for Libraries: From Fundamentals to Practical Models

هندسة أوامر الذكاء الاصطناعي للمكتبات: من الأساسيات إلى النماذج العملية

A hands-on workshop built on the trainer's book on AI prompt engineering in libraries. It takes participants from writing ad-hoc prompts to designing structured, reusable prompts that serve cataloguing, reference service, bibliometric analysis and content production inside the library.

Training areaArtificial Intelligence
LevelIntroductory to intermediate
Suggested durationTwo training days (10 hours) — 70% hands-on
Delivery languageArabic
PrerequisitesNo programming experience required; general computer use and familiarity with generative AI tools is enough

1 Workshop objectives

  • Understand how large language models work and the limits of using them in a library setting
  • Master the parts of an effective prompt: role, context, task, constraints, output format
  • Apply advanced patterns: few-shot prompting, chain of thought, stepwise analysis, templates
  • Build a unified institutional prompt library for recurring library tasks
  • Assess output quality and handle hallucination, bias and data errors
  • Document prompts, share them and version them across the team

2 What the workshop covers

  • Generative model fundamentals: capabilities, limits, core concepts
  • Anatomy of an effective prompt, and building a unified template for the library
  • Cataloguing and metadata prompts: suggested descriptors, subject headings, summaries
  • Reference service prompts: guided answers, research guides, source recommendations
  • Analysis prompts: preliminary literature reviews, study summaries, bibliometric extraction
  • Content production prompts: awareness posts, newsletters, training material
  • Quality assurance: checklists, tests, dealing with hallucination
  • Governance: privacy, copyright, disclosing the use of AI

3 Learning outcomes

By the end of the workshop, participants should be able to:

  • Write structured prompts to a unified template for any library task
  • Produce a documented library of at least ten ready prompts for their department's work
  • Apply at least three advanced patterns to real scenarios
  • Build a checklist for assessing AI output before relying on it

4 Who it is for

  • Library and information specialists across all departments
  • Cataloguers, metadata specialists and technical services staff
  • Reference service and bibliographic instruction specialists
  • Researchers and postgraduate students in library and information science
