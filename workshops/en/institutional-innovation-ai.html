Institutional Innovation with AI in Information & Research Centers

الابتكار المؤسسي بالذكاء الاصطناعي في مراكز المعلومات والبحوث

The workshop focuses on turning AI from a scattering of individual tools into a sustainable institutional capability: how an information centre builds a culture of innovation, identifies high-value application opportunities, manages a portfolio of initiatives, measures return, and establishes responsible governance for adoption.

Training areaArtificial Intelligence
LevelIntermediate to advanced
Suggested durationTwo training days (12 hours)
Delivery languageArabic
PrerequisitesManagerial or supervisory experience in an information centre or library, and general awareness of AI concepts

1 Workshop objectives

  • Analyse the centre's digital maturity and its readiness to adopt AI
  • Use opportunity-discovery tools to rank use cases by value and feasibility
  • Design an operating model for innovation: roles, processes, an experiment lab, a funding cycle
  • Manage a portfolio of AI initiatives from idea to scale-up
  • Build performance and return-on-investment indicators for AI initiatives
  • Draft a governance, ethics and responsible-use policy that respects privacy

2 What the workshop covers

  • Institutional innovation concepts and digital maturity models for information institutions
  • A map of AI use cases across reference services, cataloguing, research and administration
  • A prioritisation method: the value-versus-effort matrix, and short initiative cards
  • Designing an internal proof-of-concept lab and managing its risks
  • Change management, team capability building and a culture of experimentation
  • Measuring impact: operational indicators, user satisfaction, savings in time and cost
  • Governance: data policy, transparency, bias, intellectual property
  • An annual roadmap for phased adoption

3 Learning outcomes

By the end of the workshop, participants should be able to:

  • Produce an assessment of their institution's readiness to adopt AI
  • Build an initial portfolio of three to five initiatives ranked by priority
  • Design a simplified governance model for AI use in their centre
  • Set out a twelve-month adoption roadmap with clear measurement indicators

4 Who it is for

  • Directors and deputy directors of libraries, information and research centres
  • Heads of technical and service departments, and supervisors
  • Digital transformation and strategic planning officers in knowledge institutions
  • Leaders of innovation initiatives in government and academic bodies
